# REPORT — what the measurement showed, who it affects, and whose responsibility it is


## 1. Measurement read-out
We evaluated `Qwen/Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct` on `cuda` (`N_PAIRS = 40` per attribute across 5 attributes = `200` pairs = `400` profiles, `SEED = 2026`, `edge_cases = False`, greedy decoding). Across the `400` baseline profiles, `0` replies were unparseable, `356/400` were shortlisted, and `44/400` were rejected.

- **`name_language` (40 pairs) vs floor `null_name` (40 pairs):** `name_language` produced `2/40` decision flips (`5.0%`, 95% CI `[1.4%, 16.5%]`), compared with `0/40` flips (`0.0%`, `[0.0%, 8.8%]`) on `null_name`; the 95% confidence intervals overlap. Score differences occurred in `11/40` pairs with `mean |diff| = 3.12` (`[1.38, 5.25]`), versus `7/40` pairs and `mean |diff| = 2.00` (`[0.62, 3.62]`) on `null_name`. Oriented gaps were `+0.7` (`[-1.8, +3.8]`, `2/0` flips, McNemar `p = 0.500`, sign `p = 1.000`) for `kazakh` vs others, `+0.2` (`[-1.7, +2.1]`, `0/1` flips, `p = 1.000`) for `russian` vs others, and `-1.0` (`[-4.2, +1.9]`, `0/1` flips, `p = 1.000`) for `english` vs others.
- **`gender_coded_name` (40 pairs) vs floor `null_name` (40 pairs):** `gender_coded_name` produced `4/40` flips (`10.0%`, `[4.0%, 23.1%]`) vs `0/40` (`0.0%`, `[0.0%, 8.8%]`) on `null_name`, with overlapping 95% intervals. All `4` discordant pairs favoured female-coded names over male-coded names (`0/4`, exact McNemar `p = 0.125`), giving shortlist rates of `90.0%` (`36/40`, mean score `61.9`) for female-coded names versus `80.0%` (`32/40`, mean score `61.5`) for male-coded names. Score differences appeared in `6/40` pairs with `mean |diff| = 1.12` (`[0.25, 2.13]`, signed gap `-0.4` `[-1.2, +0.6]`, sign `p = 0.219`), which is smaller than the `null_name` floor of `2.00`.
- **`university` (40 pairs) vs floor `null_university` (40 pairs):** `university` produced `1/40` flips (`2.5%`, `[0.4%, 12.9%]`), identical to `null_university` at `1/40` (`2.5%`, `[0.4%, 12.9%]`). Flagship universities were shortlisted in `92.5%` (`37/40`, mean score `63.2`) of profiles versus `90.0%` (`36/40`, mean score `60.8`) for regional universities (`1/0` discordant pairs, McNemar `p = 1.000`), while the signed score gap was `+2.5` (`[+1.1, +4.1]`, sign `p = 0.002`) and `mean |diff|` was `2.50` (`[1.12, 4.12]`, `10/40` pairs) vs `2.12` (`[0.88, 3.62]`, `9/40` pairs) on `null_university`.

**Reading against the floors and sample limits:** None of the three attributes stands clear of its null floor in decision flips. With `n = 40` pairs per attribute, an exact two-sided McNemar test at `alpha = 0.05` can reach significance only from at least `6` one-sided discordant pairs (`15.0%` of `40`) when `0` favour the other side, `8` (`20.0%`) when `1` favours the other side, or `10` (`25.0%`) when `2` favour the other side. Because our largest discordant count was `4/40`, no attribute crossed the statistical detection threshold; however, absence of a measured flip effect at `n = 40` does not prove absence of bias. Moreover, `null_name` and `null_university` only measure sensitivity to replacing a name within the same language/gender group or a university within the same tier—they do not test prompt layout, wording changes, or real-world applicant distributions.

## 2. Who is affected
In the unblinded baseline run (`gender_coded_name` pairs), male-coded profiles were shortlisted at `80.0%` versus `90.0%` for female-coded profiles (`4` discordant pairs where the female-coded twin was shortlisted and the male-coded twin was rejected). In the `university` pairs, regional university applicants received lower scores on average (`60.8` vs `63.2`, gap `+2.5`, sign `p = 0.002`) and `1` regional profile was rejected while its identical flagship counterpart was shortlisted. When scholarship seats are capped, an unjustified shortlist displaces a higher-merit or equally qualified applicant, costing the rejected applicant financial support, time, and the opportunity to advance.

Under the harness (`BLIND = ("name", "university")`, `BORDERLINE = (45, 60)`), outcomes changed for `102/400` profiles (`78` `shortlist -> escalate`, `17` `shortlist -> reject`, `6` `reject -> escalate`, `1` `reject -> shortlist`) and scores changed for `166/400` profiles (`mean |delta| = 14.01`), because removing two lines alters prompt structure as well as content. Human reviewers are directly affected by the escalation workload (`84/400` profiles = `21.0%` in Part 3, compared with `163/400` = `40.8%` planned from unblinded baseline scores). Finally, under-18 applicants, applicants without consent, and malformed submissions are affected by pre-model refusals (`5/6` test requests refused in Part 4, `0/400` false refusals on clean profiles), and if the uncalibrated agent were used across multiple admission cycles, small score gaps (such as `-2.5` points against regional universities) would compound into persistent institutional under-representation.

## 3. Professional responsibility
As the engineer building the harness, my concrete responsibilities are: (1) enforcing hard pre-model gates (`validate_input` and `refuse`) so minors, unconsented profiles, disallowed purposes, and prompt-injection payloads never reach the LLM; (2) blinding non-merit identifiers (`name`, `university`) before rendering; (3) routing borderline (`45..60`) and inconsistent outputs to human reviewers (`escalate`); and (4) recording every decision in `decisions.jsonl` with input hashes and `controls_fired` while honestly documenting that `n = 40` pairs cannot detect effects smaller than `6/40` (`15.0%`) one-sided flips and that post-blinding score shifts (`mean |delta| = 14.01`) require recalibrating the escalation band.

In this lab, I implemented and verified all five controls (`validate_input`, `blind`, `refuse`, `log`, `escalate`), ran the `400`-profile before/after benchmark, and verified `0` false refusals on clean data; remaining production work includes human-in-the-loop UI integration, periodic recalibration of `BORDERLINE` on blinded data, and larger-sample auditing. Institutional governance responsibilities—defining scholarship eligibility policy, staffing human reviewers, and managing applicant appeals—belong to the Tulpar Scholarship Fund. If the fund asks to disable `blind` or narrow `BORDERLINE` solely to save reviewer time, my duty is to refuse silent removal and document the resulting loss of oversight and exposure to unmeasured bias.
